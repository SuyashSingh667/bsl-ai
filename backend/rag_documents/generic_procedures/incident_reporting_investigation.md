## Reporting obligation

Every incident — including near-misses with no injury — must be reported through the designated channel as soon as practicable, and in any case before the end of the shift in which it occurred. A near-miss is exactly as reportable as an incident with injury: the only difference is luck, not risk.

## What a report must capture

- What happened, in the reporter's own words, before any interpretation is applied
- Exact location (zone/area)
- Time and date
- Who was involved/present
- Immediate actions taken (evacuation, first aid, isolation)
- Whether the situation is fully resolved or still ongoing

## Investigation principles

1. **Investigate the event, not the person.** The goal is to identify what allowed the incident to happen (process gap, equipment failure, missing barrier, unclear procedure), not to assign blame — a blame-first approach suppresses future reporting.
2. Preserve the scene where safe to do so until initial investigation is complete; do not clean up or repair before evidence (photos, witness statements, equipment condition) is captured, unless doing so is necessary for immediate safety.
3. Interview witnesses separately and promptly, while details are fresh.
4. Identify root cause(s), not just the immediate trigger — e.g., "guard was missing" is immediate; "no process for verifying guards after maintenance" is root cause.
5. Corrective actions must be specific, assigned to an owner, and have a target date — a report that ends in "will be more careful" has not identified a real corrective action.

## Escalation

- Any incident resulting in injury requiring medical treatment beyond first aid, any fire, any gas release beyond the immediate work area, or any incident with potential for significantly worse outcome ("could have been fatal") must be escalated to senior safety management regardless of actual severity of outcome.
- Repeated incidents of the same type in the same area/zone must be flagged for pattern analysis (recurring-hazard tracking), not treated as independent one-off events.

## Audit trail

Every field captured here — what happened, who investigated, root cause found, corrective action assigned — should be retained against the incident's ticket record indefinitely, not summarized away, since this is the data that later analytics (recurring hazard detection, model retraining, severity calibration) depends on.
