## Core principle

No one works on or near electrical equipment that could be energized without that equipment first being isolated, locked, and tagged by an authorized person — no exceptions for "it'll only take a minute."

## If you witness an electrical incident (shock, arc flash, exposed live wiring)

1. **Do not touch the person or the equipment.** A person in contact with live equipment can pass current through anyone who touches them.
2. Isolate power at the nearest safe disconnect/breaker if you can do so without contacting the person or equipment. If unsure, don't attempt it — wait for a qualified person.
3. Once power is confirmed off, check the person for breathing/responsiveness and begin first aid/CPR if trained; call for medical help immediately regardless of how minor the shock appears — electrical injury can cause internal damage not visible externally.
4. Do not restore power until the area is fully assessed by a qualified electrical safety officer.

## Lockout-Tagout (LOTO) procedure for maintenance/repair work

1. **Notify** affected personnel that equipment will be shut down and locked out.
2. **Shut down** the equipment through its normal stopping procedure.
3. **Isolate** — disconnect the equipment from all energy sources (electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, stored energy).
4. **Lock** — apply a personal lock to each isolation point. Each worker involved applies their own lock; only that worker may remove it.
5. **Tag** — attach a danger tag identifying who applied the lock, when, and why. A danger tag identifies equipment that must not be operated; it is never used intercheably with an informational tag.
6. **Verify** — attempt to start the equipment using normal controls to confirm isolation is effective, then return controls to the "off" position.
7. Only after all of the above is complete may work begin.
8. To restore: remove tools, ensure the area is clear, remove locks (only the worker who applied a lock removes it), and restore energy following the equipment's normal start-up sequence.

## General electrical safety rules

- Only authorized/trained personnel may work on electrical equipment above extra-low voltage.
- Never use water or a general-purpose extinguisher on an electrical fire — use CO₂ or dry powder.
- Treat all circuits as live until proven otherwise by testing with a calibrated instrument, not by visual inspection.
- Report any exposed wiring, damaged insulation, or unusual smell/heat from electrical equipment immediately — do not wait for it to fail.
