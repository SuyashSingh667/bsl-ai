## General principle

PPE is the last line of defense, used together with (not instead of) engineering controls and safe procedures. Required PPE depends on the task and area, not personal preference.

## Baseline PPE (any general plant-floor area)

- Safety helmet
- Safety footwear (steel/composite toe, per area hazard)
- High-visibility clothing
- Safety glasses/goggles

## Task/area-specific additions

| Area/task | Additional PPE |
|---|---|
| Hot-process areas (furnaces, casting) | Heat-resistant clothing, face shield, heat-resistant gloves |
| Gas-handling areas | Appropriate respiratory protection rated for the specific gas (never a generic dust mask) |
| Chemical handling (acids, oils) | Chemical-resistant gloves, apron, face shield |
| Electrical work | Insulated gloves rated for the voltage, arc-rated clothing where applicable |
| Confined space entry | Supplied-air respirator or SCBA, harness with retrieval line, gas monitor |
| Noisy areas | Hearing protection |
| Overhead/falling-object risk | Hard hat rated for the specific risk, and physical barriers where feasible |

## If a worker is found without required PPE

1. The task must stop immediately until correct PPE is available and worn — this is not a "finish the task first" situation, since the exposure is ongoing for every minute the task continues.
2. Establish whether PPE was unavailable (a supply/access failure — a systemic issue) or the worker chose not to wear it (a compliance issue) — the response differs, but the immediate stop-work does not.
3. Log the incident regardless of cause, since repeated PPE gaps in the same area/task are a leading indicator worth tracking over time (see recurring-hazard analytics).

## PPE maintenance

- Damaged, expired, or contaminated PPE must be replaced, not repaired for continued use, except where the manufacturer explicitly permits repair.
- Respiratory protection must be fit-tested to the individual wearer; a respirator that doesn't seal properly provides a false sense of protection.
