## If mechanical equipment fails or behaves unexpectedly

1. Stop the equipment using the nearest emergency stop if it is safe to reach it without entering the danger zone (moving parts, pinch points, falling material).
2. Do not attempt to physically intervene (freeing a jam, reaching into moving parts) — this is a leading cause of severe injury in mechanical failures.
3. Clear the immediate area of personnel and warn others approaching.
4. Report the failure through the designated channel, noting what the equipment was doing, what changed, and whether anyone was in the danger zone at the time.

## Permit-to-Work — required before entering or working on isolated equipment

1. Equipment must be brought to a safe state and isolated from all energy sources (mechanical, electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, gravitational — e.g., suspended loads).
2. Moving parts must be fully stopped and, where relevant, physically blocked against unexpected movement (not relying on isolation alone).
3. A permit is issued naming the work, the people authorized, the isolation points confirmed, and the time window.
4. Guards and safety interlocks removed for the work must be logged and reinstated before the permit is closed — equipment must never be returned to service with a guard missing.
5. Before restart: confirm all personnel and tools are clear, guards are reinstated, and the permit is formally closed by an authorized person.

## General mechanical safety rules

- Never bypass, disable, or defeat a machine guard or interlock for convenience.
- Report unusual vibration, noise, heat, or smell from rotating/moving equipment immediately rather than waiting for a scheduled inspection.
- Loose clothing, jewelry, and unrestrained long hair must be secured before working near moving machinery.
- Only operate equipment you are trained and authorized to operate.
