## Why this is not "just a fire"

Molten metal presents two distinct hazard mechanisms beyond simple heat: severe contact burns on a timescale of milliseconds, and a **steam explosion** if the metal contacts water or moisture — molten metal flashes trapped water to steam almost instantly, and the resulting pressure wave and metal splatter can travel much further than the spill itself. This is why "is there water nearby" is a higher-priority question than for a normal fire.

## Immediate actions (worker who witnesses a spill)

1. Move away immediately, in a direction away from the spill's flow path — molten metal follows gravity and floor contours, so uphill/upslope is generally safer than lateral movement.
2. **Do not attempt to apply water, or anything containing moisture, to molten metal or a fire involving it** — this is the single most important rule and the most common cause of secondary steam-explosion injuries.
3. Warn anyone downslope of the spill while withdrawing.
4. Report immediately: source (if known), whether it's still flowing, and whether anyone is in the flow path.

## For the response team

1. Do not approach until the source has been isolated (furnace tilted back, ladle repositioned, tap hole plugged) by qualified operating personnel — a spill with an active, ongoing source is not yet a static hazard.
2. Confirm no water sources (hoses, drains, puddles, damp material) are in or near the spill's path before any cooling/containment action.
3. Use dry sand or purpose-built containment material to redirect or dam the flow — never water-based suppression.
4. Establish an exclusion zone accounting for splatter/spray, not just the visible pool.
5. Approach for rescue only in full heat-resistant PPE; a person who has suffered molten-metal contact burns needs immediate specialized medical evacuation — do not attempt extended on-site treatment beyond initial cooling with dry methods and covering the wound.

## After containment

- The area must be inspected for refractory or structural damage before normal operation resumes — a spill often indicates an upstream containment failure (cracked ladle lining, failed tap-hole clay) that will recur if not addressed, not just cleaned up.
- Full incident report including source, containment failure mode if identified, and whether any water-contact steam event occurred.
