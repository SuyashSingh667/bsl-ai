## Why this is handled separately from a gas leak

A liquid spill's primary risks are direct contact (skin/eye burns), runoff/drain contamination, and — for volatile chemicals — vapor buildup in the immediate area, rather than wide gas dispersion. Response therefore centers on containment and decontamination, not evacuation-radius management, though a large volatile spill can still require area evacuation.

## Immediate actions (worker who witnesses a spill)

1. Avoid direct contact — do not step in the spill or attempt to stop it with bare hands or unprotected clothing.
2. Move away from the immediate area, especially if there is any smell, visible vapor, or irritation felt (eyes, throat, skin) — these are signs of a chemical with vapor hazard, not just a contact hazard.
3. Prevent the spill from reaching a floor drain if this can be done safely from a distance (e.g., placing absorbent barrier material without entering the spill) — many industrial chemicals are environmentally hazardous and drain contamination creates a second incident.
4. Report immediately: the substance if known (check container labeling from a safe distance), approximate quantity, and whether anyone has been in direct contact.

## If skin or eye contact has occurred

1. Flush the affected area with water immediately and continuously for at least 15 minutes, using an eyewash station or safety shower if available — do not wait for instructions before starting this.
2. Remove contaminated clothing while flushing.
3. Seek medical attention regardless of how minor the exposure appears — some chemicals cause delayed-onset injury.

## For the response team

1. Identify the substance via its safety data sheet before selecting containment/cleanup method — the correct response (absorbent type, neutralizing agent, PPE) depends on the specific chemical's properties, and using the wrong method can worsen the hazard (e.g., certain absorbents react with certain acids).
2. Isolate the source (valve, container) if this can be done safely.
3. Contain the spill with appropriate absorbent material, working from the outside edge inward to prevent spreading.
4. Ventilate the area if there is any vapor concern, once contact/ignition risks have been assessed.

## After containment

- Dispose of contaminated absorbent material and any affected PPE according to hazardous-waste procedure — not as regular waste.
- Full incident report including substance, quantity, cause (container failure, transfer error, valve failure), and whether any drain/environmental release occurred.
