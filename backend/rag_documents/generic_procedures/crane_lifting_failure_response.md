## Immediate actions (worker who witnesses a lifting failure — dropped load, uncontrolled swing, visible equipment damage)

1. Move away from underneath and away from the load's swing path immediately — a load that has slipped or a cable that has failed can move unpredictably, not just straight down.
2. Do not attempt to steady, catch, or manually guide a swinging or falling load under any circumstances.
3. Warn anyone else in the area while withdrawing.
4. Report immediately: what was being lifted, whether the load is still suspended or has fallen, and whether anyone is under or near it.

## For the crane operator / response team

1. If the load is still suspended and controllable, lower it to the ground in the safest available clear area rather than leaving it suspended — a damaged crane holding a suspended load is an ongoing hazard, not a resolved one.
2. If the crane itself shows signs of structural failure (visible cable damage, boom deformation, unusual noise under load), stop operation and do not attempt further lifts until inspected.
3. Establish an exclusion zone under and around the load's full possible swing radius, not just its current position.
4. If anyone is trapped or struck, do not move heavy components off them without appropriate lifting equipment and trained personnel — improvised lifting can cause further injury.

## After the incident

- The crane must be taken out of service and inspected by a qualified person before any further use — do not resume lifting operations based on a visual check alone.
- Full incident report including load weight/type, apparent failure point (cable, hook, sling, structural), and whether load-rating limits were being observed at the time.

## Prevention principles worth noting in any investigation

- Load weight must be confirmed against the crane/sling's rated capacity before every lift, not assumed.
- No one should stand or pass underneath a suspended load, regardless of how routine the lift appears to be.
- Regular inspection of slings, hooks, and cables for wear must precede use, not follow an incident.
