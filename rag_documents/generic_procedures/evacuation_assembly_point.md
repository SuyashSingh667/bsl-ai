## When to evacuate

- Immediately on hearing the evacuation alarm, or on instruction from an emergency responder/safety officer — do not wait for confirmation of the cause.
- Immediately on personally detecting a credible immediate danger (active fire, strong gas odor with symptoms, visible structural failure), even before an alarm sounds — do not wait to "make sure."

## During evacuation

1. Stop work, leave tools/equipment safely (do not attempt to shut down equipment yourself unless that is your assigned emergency role).
2. Move to the nearest designated exit, not necessarily the one you normally use.
3. Walk, do not run — moving quickly but under control avoids secondary injuries.
4. Do not use elevators.
5. Assist anyone with mobility difficulty if you can do so without significantly delaying your own evacuation; otherwise report their location to responders.
6. Proceed directly to your area's designated assembly point — not to the parking lot, not to watch from a distance.

## At the assembly point

1. Report to your area supervisor/marshal for headcount.
2. Remain at the assembly point until an "all clear" is given by the incident commander — not until the situation merely looks calmer.
3. Report any missing colleague to the marshal immediately; do not attempt to go back in to look for them yourself.

## Zone-specific consideration for this platform

Since the spatial impact engine already computes which zones fall within a hazard's primary/secondary band, evacuation priority should follow that same ordering — zones in the primary band evacuate first and are verified first, before extending attention to secondary-band zones. A civilian-exposure alert (hazard reaching the township boundary) should additionally trigger external notification, not just internal evacuation.

## Re-entry

No one re-enters an evacuated area until the designated safety authority issues a formal all-clear, based on confirmed hazard resolution — not based on the alarm stopping or visible calm.
