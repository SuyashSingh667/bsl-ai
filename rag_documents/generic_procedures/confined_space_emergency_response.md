## The single most important rule

**If someone collapses inside a confined space, do not go in after them without a self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA) and a retrieval plan, even if it means waiting the extra time to get it.** Whatever atmosphere caused the first person to collapse — oxygen deficiency, toxic gas, gas displacement — will affect an untrained rescuer exactly the same way, usually faster, since they enter without warning. A large share of confined-space fatalities are rescuers, not the original entrant. Acting on adrenaline instead of protocol here routinely turns a one-person emergency into a multiple-fatality one.

## Immediate actions (worker who witnesses a collapse or emergency inside a confined space)

1. **Do not enter.** Call for help immediately.
2. If a ventilation fan is running, keep it running; if one is available and not running, activate it if this can be done from outside the space.
3. If a rescue harness and retrieval line are attached to the person and can be operated from outside the space without entering, use them.
4. Alert the designated confined-space rescue team / emergency responders immediately — this is not a standard first-aid situation.

## Before entry by rescue personnel

1. Atmosphere must be tested (oxygen level, toxic gas concentration, flammability) before anyone enters, including rescuers.
2. Entry requires appropriate respiratory protection matched to the confirmed or suspected hazard — supplied air or SCBA, not a filtering respirator, unless atmosphere testing has specifically ruled out oxygen deficiency.
3. An attendant must remain outside the space at all times during entry, maintaining communication and ready to initiate retrieval.
4. Retrieval equipment (harness, winch/tripod) should be used to extract the casualty without additional personnel entering, wherever the space's geometry allows.

## After extraction

- Move the casualty to fresh air and begin appropriate first aid/resuscitation; oxygen-deficiency and toxic-gas exposure can have effects that aren't visually obvious immediately, so medical evacuation is required regardless of how the person appears on removal.
- Do not permit re-entry by anyone, for any reason (including retrieving equipment) until the space is re-tested and confirmed safe.

## Prevention

Entry into any confined space requires a permit confirming atmospheric testing, ventilation, attendant presence, and rescue-equipment readiness *before* entry — this procedure exists specifically because the emergency scenario above is preventable when entry protocol is followed.
