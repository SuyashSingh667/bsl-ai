## If a slip/fall occurs

1. Do not move an injured person unless they are in immediate further danger (e.g., in a traffic path) — moving someone with a potential spinal or fracture injury can worsen it.
2. Call for medical assistance; keep the person still and calm while waiting.
3. Cordon off or mark the hazard that caused the fall (spill, obstruction, damaged flooring) immediately so it doesn't cause a second incident while attention is on the injured worker.
4. Report the incident, including what caused the fall (surface condition, lighting, obstruction, footwear) — this detail is what makes the report useful for prevention, not just record-keeping.

## Routine housekeeping requirements

- Spills must be cleaned up or barricaded/signed immediately — not left for the next shift.
- Walkways must be kept clear of tools, cables, and materials; temporary cables crossing a walkway must be covered or routed overhead.
- Adequate lighting must be maintained in all walkways and work areas.
- Flooring defects (cracks, loose tiles, uneven transitions) must be reported and repaired, not worked around indefinitely.
- Handrails on stairs and elevated platforms must be intact and not used for anything other than their intended purpose (no hanging tools or materials on them).

## Areas requiring specific attention

- Areas near washrooms, drinking-water points, and building entrances (moisture tracked in from outside/rain).
- Loading/unloading areas where oil, grease, or material spillage is common.
- Stairwells and elevated walkways, where a fall has more severe consequences than on level ground.

## Prevention over reaction

A pattern of slip/fall reports in the same location is a signal that the underlying condition (poor drainage, inadequate flooring, recurring spill source) needs fixing — not that the same spot needs repeated cleanup after each incident.
