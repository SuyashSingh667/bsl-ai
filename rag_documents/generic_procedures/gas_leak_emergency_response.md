## Immediate actions (worker who detects/suspects a gas leak)

1. **Do not attempt to locate the source or investigate further.** Move away from the suspected area immediately, moving upwind if direction is known.
2. **Do not operate electrical switches, create sparks, or use open flame** in or near the suspected area — this includes light switches, vehicle ignitions, and mobile phones if the leak is suspected to be a flammable gas.
3. Warn nearby personnel verbally while withdrawing, if it is safe to do so without delaying your own evacuation.
4. Report the leak immediately through the designated reporting channel (this platform, radio, or the nearest emergency phone) — state location, what you observed (smell, hissing sound, visible vapor cloud), and whether anyone is symptomatic.
5. Proceed to the nearest designated assembly point unless instructed otherwise.

## For the responding team / gas services

1. Do not permit entry to the affected area until tested and confirmed safe by qualified personnel with appropriate gas detection equipment.
2. Identify wind direction and establish a safe approach from upwind.
3. Isolate the source if it can be done remotely/safely (e.g., closing an isolation valve) — never attempt manual isolation without appropriate PPE (respiratory protection rated for the specific gas, per its safety data sheet).
4. Establish an exclusion zone based on the gas's known hazard radius; expand if concentration readings indicate.
5. Do not extinguish a leaking gas fire unless the leak itself can be stopped — an extinguished but still-leaking source can reignite explosively or accumulate into a larger vapor cloud.

## Symptoms indicating exposure requiring immediate medical attention

Dizziness, headache, nausea, confusion, difficulty breathing, chest tightness, loss of coordination, or loss of consciousness. Any of these in a worker who was in or near the affected area warrants immediate medical evacuation, not a wait-and-see approach.

## After the area is declared safe

- Do not resume normal operations until the isolation/repair is verified and a re-entry authorization is issued by the responsible safety authority.
- File a full incident report including duration of exposure (if any), number of people in the area, and corrective action taken.
